The wizarding world exists alongside and hidden within the Muggle (non-magical) world, maintained through the International Statute of Wizarding Secrecy established in 1692. Witches and wizards have their own government, economy, media, and social institutions, all carefully concealed from the non-magical population through a combination of Memory Charms, enchantments, and the Ministry of Magic's dedicated Obliviator squads.

The Ministry of Magic is the governing body of the British wizarding community, headquartered in London beneath the streets, accessible through various concealed entrances including a visitors' entrance via a red telephone box (dial 62442, which spells MAGIC). The Ministry is organised into departments including the Department of Magical Law Enforcement (the largest), the Department of Mysteries (conducting secret research into fundamental aspects of magic such as love, death, time, and thought), the Department for the Regulation and Control of Magical Creatures, the Department of International Magical Cooperation, and the Department of Magical Games and Sports. The Minister for Magic heads the government — notable ministers include Cornelius Fudge (who refused to believe Voldemort had returned and waged a smear campaign against Dumbledore and Harry), Rufus Scrimgeour (who prioritised appearances over effective action), and Kingsley Shacklebolt (who reformed the Ministry after the Second Wizarding War).

The Auror Office, part of the Department of Magical Law Enforcement, employs highly trained dark wizard catchers. Becoming an Auror requires top grades in Potions, Defence Against the Dark Arts, Transfiguration, Charms, and Herbology, followed by three years of additional training. Famous Aurors include Alastor "Mad-Eye" Moody (who filled half the cells in Azkaban and was famously paranoid — "CONSTANT VIGILANCE!"), Nymphadora Tonks (a Metamorphmagus who could change her appearance at will), and eventually Harry Potter and Ron Weasley, who both joined the Auror Office after the Second Wizarding War.

Diagon Alley is the main commercial hub of wizarding Britain, hidden behind the Leaky Cauldron pub in London (invisible to Muggles). The entrance is accessed by tapping a specific brick in the wall behind the pub. Diagon Alley is where wizarding families purchase school supplies, including Ollivanders (wandmakers since 382 BC, whose shop bears the motto "Makers of Fine Wands since 382 B.C."), Flourish and Blotts (bookshop), Madam Malkin's Robes for All Occasions, the Apothecary, Eeylops Owl Emporium, and Quality Quidditch Supplies. Weasleys' Wizard Wheezes, the joke shop opened by Fred and George Weasley at 93 Diagon Alley using Harry's Triwizard Tournament winnings, became an enormous success selling products like Extendable Ears, Skiving Snackboxes, Pygmy Puffs, and Peruvian Instant Darkness Powder.

Knockturn Alley branches off from Diagon Alley and is devoted to the Dark Arts. Borgin and Burkes, its most notable shop, deals in dark artefacts and objects with powerful and dangerous magical properties. Tom Riddle worked at Borgin and Burkes briefly after leaving Hogwarts, where he acquired Hufflepuff's cup and Slytherin's locket from elderly collectors before murdering them and turning the objects into Horcruxes.

Gringotts Wizarding Bank, run by goblins, is the only bank in the wizarding world. Located in Diagon Alley, the snowy-white marble building extends deep underground, with high-security vaults reached by cart along a network of underground railway tracks. Security measures include Thief's Downfall (a waterfall that washes away all enchantments and disguises), dragons guarding the deepest vaults, and various curses and enchantments. The bank's motto, inscribed on the inner doors, warns: "Enter, stranger, but take heed / Of what awaits the sin of greed." Harry, Ron, and Hermione broke into Gringotts to retrieve Hufflepuff's cup from the Lestrange vault — the only known successful break-in in the bank's history — escaping on the back of the blind dragon that guarded the deepest vaults.

Hogsmeade is the only entirely non-Muggle settlement in Britain, located near Hogwarts. Third-year students and above can visit on designated weekends with a signed permission form. Popular establishments include the Three Broomsticks (run by Madam Rosmerta, serving butterbeer — a mildly alcoholic, butterscotch-flavoured drink that is the most popular beverage in the wizarding world), the Hog's Head (a rougher pub run by Aberforth Dumbledore, Albus's brother), Honeydukes (a sweet shop selling Chocolate Frogs, Bertie Bott's Every Flavour Beans, Pepper Imps, and other magical confections), Zonko's Joke Shop, and the Shrieking Shack (reputed to be the most haunted building in Britain, though the screams heard there were actually Remus Lupin during his werewolf transformations).

Azkaban is the wizarding prison, located on a tiny island in the middle of the North Sea. For centuries, it was guarded by Dementors, which kept the prisoners in a state of constant despair. Many inmates went insane within weeks, and few survived long sentences. Sirius Black was the first known person to escape from Azkaban, managing it by transforming into his Animagus dog form — Dementors, who sense emotions rather than physical forms, could not detect his simpler animal feelings. After the Second Wizarding War, Kingsley Shacklebolt removed the Dementors from Azkaban and replaced them with Auror guards as part of his Ministry reforms.

The wizarding economy uses a currency of gold Galleons, silver Sickles, and bronze Knuts. There are 17 Sickles to a Galleon and 29 Knuts to a Sickle. A Galleon is roughly equivalent to five British pounds. The Daily Prophet, the main wizarding newspaper, is delivered by owl and costs five Knuts. Other media include The Quibbler (edited by Xenophilius Lovegood, publishing conspiracy theories and unusual stories — though it gained credibility when it published Harry's interview about Voldemort's return) and the Wizarding Wireless Network (WWN), a magical radio service. During the Second Wizarding War, a pirate radio station called Potterwatch provided real news, hosted by Lee Jordan under the codename "River" with regular contributors Fred Weasley ("Rapier"), Kingsley Shacklebolt ("Royal"), and Remus Lupin ("Romulus").

The wizarding world communicates primarily through owl post, with owls trained to find any recipient. The Floo Network connects fireplaces throughout the wizarding world — witches and wizards can travel by throwing Floo powder into a fireplace and clearly stating their destination, or communicate by placing their head in the fire. Howlers are enchanted red letters that, if not opened promptly, explode and shout their message at full volume — typically sent by furious parents. Ron received a memorable Howler from Molly Weasley after flying the enchanted Ford Anglia to Hogwarts.
