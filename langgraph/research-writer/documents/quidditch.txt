Quidditch is the most popular sport in the wizarding world, played on broomsticks high above the ground. The game is fast, dangerous, and deeply beloved — professional Quidditch matches draw enormous crowds, and the Quidditch World Cup is the most attended sporting event in the magical community. The sport's origins date back to the 11th century, and its rules have been documented in "Quidditch Through the Ages" by Kennilworthy Whisp.

Each Quidditch team consists of seven players. Three Chasers handle the Quaffle (a red, football-sized ball) and attempt to throw it through one of three goal hoops at the opposing end of the pitch, scoring ten points per goal. One Keeper guards their team's three hoops — the position is roughly equivalent to a goalkeeper. Two Beaters carry short, heavy bats and are responsible for the Bludgers — two enchanted iron balls that fly around the pitch attempting to knock players off their brooms. Beaters aim the Bludgers toward opposing players and away from their own teammates. The final position, and the most glamorous, is the Seeker. The Seeker's sole job is to catch the Golden Snitch, a tiny, walnut-sized gold ball with silver wings that flies at tremendous speed and is designed to be nearly impossible to see. Catching the Snitch is worth 150 points and ends the game. In most matches, the team that catches the Snitch wins, but not always — if the opposing team is more than 150 points ahead, catching the Snitch ends the game but loses it.

The Golden Snitch replaced the earlier Golden Snidget, a small, round, fast-flying bird that was hunted to near-extinction because of its use in Quidditch. The invention of the enchanted metal Snitch by Bowman Wright of Godric's Hollow saved the Snidget from extinction. Snitches have flesh memories, meaning they remember the touch of the first person to handle them — this property was used by Dumbledore to conceal the Resurrection Stone inside the first Snitch Harry ever caught, which would only open when pressed to his lips.

Broomsticks are both everyday transport and finely tuned sporting equipment. Notable broom models include the Nimbus 2000 (state of the art in Harry's first year, given to him by Professor McGonagall after she witnessed his natural flying ability during a confrontation with Draco Malfoy), the Nimbus 2001 (purchased by Lucius Malfoy for the entire Slytherin team to secure Draco's position as Seeker), and the Firebolt (the finest racing broom in the world, capable of going from 0 to 150 miles per hour in ten seconds, given to Harry by Sirius Black). First-year students at Hogwarts traditionally are not allowed their own broomsticks, though an exception was made for Harry due to his extraordinary talent.

Harry Potter became the youngest Seeker in a century when he joined the Gryffindor team in his first year. His natural flying ability was evident from his very first flying lesson, when he executed a fifty-foot dive to catch Neville Longbottom's Remembrall — a feat that caught McGonagall's attention and led to his recruitment rather than expulsion. Harry played Seeker for Gryffindor for most of his time at Hogwarts, missing some matches due to detentions, injuries, and bans. He was made Quidditch Captain in his sixth year.

Notable Quidditch matches at Hogwarts include Harry's first match (where his broom was jinxed by Quirrell, though Snape attempted counter-jinxes, and Harry caught the Snitch in his mouth), the match where rogue Bludger broke Harry's arm (enchanted by Dobby in an attempt to protect Harry, after which Gilderoy Lockhart accidentally removed all the bones in Harry's arm instead of mending it), and numerous fiercely contested Gryffindor vs. Slytherin matches.

The Quidditch World Cup of 1994 (featured in "Goblet of Fire") saw Ireland defeat Bulgaria despite the Bulgarian Seeker Viktor Krum catching the Snitch. This was the rare case where catching the Snitch was not enough — Ireland's Chasers had built such a commanding lead that Krum's capture of the Snitch, while ending the game, could not overcome the deficit. Krum caught the Snitch to end the match on his own terms rather than suffer a prolonged humiliation. The match took place at a specially constructed stadium that held 100,000 witches and wizards and featured team mascots — Ireland's leprechauns showered the crowd with gold (which later vanished) and Bulgaria's Veela enchanted the male spectators with their unearthly beauty.

Professional Quidditch teams in Britain and Ireland include the Chudley Cannons (Ron Weasley's favourite team, perpetual underdogs whose motto was changed from "We shall conquer" to "Let's all just keep our fingers crossed and hope for the best"), the Holyhead Harpies (an all-female team that Ginny Weasley later played for professionally), Puddlemere United (Oliver Wood joined their reserve team after leaving Hogwarts), and the Kenmare Kestrels, Ballycastle Bats, and Pride of Portree, among others.

Quidditch fouls number seven hundred, though the full list has never been made public because, according to the Department of Magical Games and Sports, "the majority of the seven hundred fouls listed cannot occur at a game where the referee is present." Common fouls include Blagging (grabbing an opponent's broomtail), Blatching (flying with intent to collide), Cobbing (excessive use of elbows), and Stooging (more than one Chaser entering the scoring area).
