The wizarding world is home to an extraordinary variety of magical creatures, classified by the Ministry of Magic's Department for the Regulation and Control of Magical Creatures according to danger level, from harmless (X) to known wizard-killer (XXXXX). Newt Scamander's textbook "Fantastic Beasts and Where to Find Them" is the standard reference used at Hogwarts.

Dragons are among the most fearsome magical creatures, classified XXXXX. There are ten known breeds of dragon, including the Common Welsh Green, the Swedish Short-Snout, the Hungarian Horntail (considered the most dangerous, with bronze horns and spiked tail), the Norwegian Ridgeback, and the Ukrainian Ironbelly (the largest breed). Dragons are fire-breathing, enormously powerful, and nearly impossible to domesticate. Dragon breeding was outlawed by the Warlocks' Convention of 1709. Charlie Weasley works with dragons at a reserve in Romania. During the Triwizard Tournament, each champion had to retrieve a golden egg from a nesting mother dragon — Harry faced the Hungarian Horntail and outflew it on his broomstick.

Phoenixes are magnificent magical birds that burst into flame when they reach old age and are reborn from the ashes. Phoenix feathers are one of the three supreme wand cores. A phoenix's tears have powerful healing properties capable of curing even basilisk venom. Their song has magical properties that increase the courage of the pure of heart and strikes fear into the hearts of the impure. Fawkes, Albus Dumbledore's phoenix, was a loyal companion who came to Harry's aid in the Chamber of Secrets, blinding the basilisk and providing tears to heal Harry's wound. Both Harry Potter's and Lord Voldemort's wands contained a tail feather from Fawkes, making them brother wands.

Hippogriffs are proud creatures with the front body of an eagle and the hindquarters of a horse, classified XXX. They are deeply sensitive about etiquette — approaching one requires maintaining eye contact and bowing. If the hippogriff bows back, it is safe to approach. Failure to show proper respect can result in a vicious attack. Buckbeak, a hippogriff in Hagrid's Care of Magical Creatures class, slashed Draco Malfoy after Malfoy insulted him and was sentenced to death, though Harry and Hermione rescued him using a Time-Turner. Buckbeak later lived with Sirius Black and was renamed Witherwings.

Thestrals are skeletal, winged horses visible only to those who have witnessed and emotionally processed death. They are classified XXXX despite being quite gentle and intelligent. Thestrals have an excellent sense of direction and can understand verbal instructions. A herd of thestrals lives in the Forbidden Forest and pulls the Hogwarts carriages (which appear to most students to be moving on their own). Harry could see thestrals from his fifth year onward, after witnessing Cedric Diggory's death. Luna Lovegood could also see them, having witnessed her mother's death as a child.

House-elves are small, highly magical beings bound to serve wizarding families or institutions. They possess powerful magic of their own — they can Apparate where wizards cannot (including inside Hogwarts), become invisible, and perform magic without a wand. House-elves are bound by enchantment to obey their masters and can only be freed by being presented with clothing. Dobby, formerly enslaved by the Malfoy family, was freed when Harry tricked Lucius Malfoy into giving Dobby a sock. Dobby became a free elf who worked in the Hogwarts kitchens and remained fiercely loyal to Harry, ultimately sacrificing his life to rescue Harry and his friends from Malfoy Manor. Kreacher, the Black family house-elf, initially resented Harry but became loyal after Harry showed him kindness and gave him Regulus Black's locket.

Basilisks are giant serpents, classified XXXXX, capable of living for hundreds of years. They are bred by hatching a chicken's egg beneath a toad. The basilisk's gaze is instantly fatal to anyone who makes direct eye contact. Indirect eye contact (through a reflection, ghost, camera, or water) causes Petrification rather than death. Only Parselmouths — wizards who can speak Parseltongue, the language of snakes — can control a basilisk. Salazar Slytherin concealed a basilisk in the Chamber of Secrets beneath Hogwarts, where it remained for nearly a thousand years until Tom Riddle (Lord Voldemort) opened the Chamber during his time as a student and again through his diary Horcrux fifty years later. Harry destroyed the basilisk with the Sword of Gryffindor, and its venomous fangs later proved essential for destroying Horcruxes.

Dementors are among the most terrifying creatures in the wizarding world, classified XXXXX. They are dark, cloaked figures that feed on human happiness and can drain all positive emotion from their surroundings, leaving only despair and hopelessness. Their most terrible weapon is the Dementor's Kiss, which sucks out a person's soul, leaving the body alive but utterly empty. Dementors were employed as guards of Azkaban prison but proved uncontrollable and switched allegiance to Lord Voldemort during the Second Wizarding War. The only known defence against Dementors is the Patronus Charm, which conjures a silvery guardian fuelled by the caster's happiest memory. Harry's Patronus takes the form of a stag (the same as his father's Animagus form), and he learned to cast it at age thirteen under Remus Lupin's tutelage.

Centaurs are highly intelligent beings with the upper body of a human and the lower body of a horse. They live in herds in forests (including the Forbidden Forest at Hogwarts) and are skilled in archery, healing, and divination through star-gazing. Centaurs are proud and independent, resenting being classified as "beasts" by the Ministry of Magic and generally refusing to interact with wizards. Firenze was unusual among centaurs for agreeing to teach Divination at Hogwarts, which caused his herd to banish him as a traitor.
